What happened

Release 4.2 shipped clean. Then the phones went quiet.

On 12 August, Dispatch changed two things at once: it counted distance for more when choosing who to ping, and it gave each responder 60 seconds to answer instead of 90. No rollback, no alarms. A week later the complaints began.

38pings missed in the week of 10 Aug, against 2 to 6 a week before
54%of pings taken that week, down from 75 to 78%
83open support tickets, every one filed since 12 Aug
3×normal callout ticket volume reported by support from 18 Aug
12 Aug4.2 ships. Wait cut 90 → 60 s, distance weighted up.
14 AugEngineering manager asks if decliners were meant to be treated the same. No answer recorded.
18 AugSupport: tickets ~3× normal. "My phone never goes off."
26 AugStill elevated: two thirds "never goes off", one third "gone before I could answer".
28 AugNew PM gets a week to look before the team regroups.

The headline number

The weekly average recovered. The problem did not.

Acceptance fell to 54% in the week of 4.2, then climbed back to 73%. Read as a single number, that looks like a dip. Look at the misses and it is a step change that never went away.

Who it hit

Four responders went from about 12 pings a week to one.

Farlight, Meteor Mite, The Undertow and Vesper. The other twelve responders were pinged more than before. The Gale, in the same area and under the same handler as Meteor Mite, went from 13 to 21.

Why they stay quiet

A miss counts the same as a "no", and nothing ever fades.

Dispatch scores each responder on how often they take pings. Every missed ping costs 0.12. Only a taken ping adds back (0.08). Once the score is low the responder is rarely asked, so there is little chance to earn it back.

1A ping goes out to a phone.
2No answer in 60 seconds (it was 90). Counted as missed.
3Score drops 0.12, the same as saying no.
4Ranked lower, and distance now counts for more than history.
5Fewer pings reach them.
6Fewer chances to earn +0.08 back. About 7 takes in a row are needed from zero.

Being straight about it

What the evidence shows, and what it does not.

KNOWN

Shown in the data and the code

  • Missed pings jumped on 12 Aug and stayed high.
  • A miss and a "no" cost the same score, and the score never decays.
  • Four responders' scores fell to about zero and their pings with them.
  • The ping wait did shrink: about 92 s between a miss and the next ping before, about 62 s after.
  • Support tickets follow the pattern, and the largest unmatched group is the quiet phone.
NOT PROVEN

Still open

  • Why the first misses happened. Shorter wait or changed weights? The data has no answer times.
  • The idea that people who answered in 60 to 90 seconds now miss fits the code but is untested.
  • "It's seasonal": no earlier-year data to check.
  • Whether treating decliners the same was a decision. The 14 Aug question has no recorded answer.
  • What the console shows handlers like Kip today.

The fix

Let a quiet responder earn their way back.

Not a changed number. Old misses lose weight as time passes, so a rough patch fades, and the handler can see why someone is ranked where they are. Try it: this is the real prototype.

Making sure it does not happen again

Two skills that ask the awkward questions before we ship.

4.2 shipped with gaps that were easy to miss: a headline metric that hid four people, nobody owning the numbers, and a question to engineering left unanswered. The skills do not stop a release by themselves. They make those gaps show up on paper first.

SKILL 1

review-checklist: tests the brief

Run on my own brief:

✓ Success measureCounts responders pinged less than half as often.
✓ ScopeStays on score recovery.
✓ Problem firstProblem comes before the fix.
✗ OwnerNo one named as responsible.
✗ Ready for managementEnds in questions, not a decision.
✗ Plain languageScoring values up front.
SKILL 2

qa-testing: tests the build

I approve acceptance criteria drawn from the brief, then the skill runs them in a real browser and writes a report. On the prototype:

18 / 19criteria passed, including every "must"
1caught a console that did not show Kip's responders; fixed and retested

Its checks include the edges: the 0.00 floor, the 1.00 ceiling, and that a miss costs exactly what a "no" costs.

Prompt I wrote (#6)
Make a skill called qa-testing. When I point to the product brief that outlines the requirements & the staging environment where the code build against it is housed, it creates robust acceptance criteria’s, validate’s them with me, performs the tests & generates a document with all findings.

What happens next

Open items, in the order I would tackle them.

Before the brief goes to Helen

  • Name an owner for the work.
  • Replace the closing questions with one clear decision to approve.
  • Move scoring values out of the opening and say what Vesper and Kip actually experience.

To close the open questions

  • Ask the staff engineer and Ravi for answer times, so we can tell the 60-second wait from the weight change.
  • Get the 14 Aug question answered: was treating decliners the same a decision?
  • Check what the console shows handlers today before promising a "recovering" marker.

Decisions to settle

  • How fast should a score recover, and what stops a responder who really has stopped taking work?
  • Who owns the per-responder numbers: Ravi, support, or Dispatch?

Keep using the skills

  • Run review-checklist on every brief before it is shared.
  • Run qa-testing on every build before it leaves staging.
