Act II · Define · Chapter 4 · X-Ray Vision

A miss counts the same as a "no". Nothing ever fades.

NarratorTo see why they stayed quiet, we read the code. Every missed ping costs 0.12, the same as a refusal. Only a taken ping adds back, and nothing wears off.

1A ping goes out to a phone.
2No answer in 60 seconds (it was 90). Counted as missed.
3Score drops 0.12, the same as saying no.
4Ranked lower, and distance now counts for more than history.
5Fewer pings reach them.
6Fewer chances to earn +0.08 back. About 7 takes in a row from zero.

Act II · Define · The problem statement

How might we let a quiet responder earn their way back?

NarratorDefine means choosing one problem and being honest about what we still don't know. Here is the problem, and here is where the evidence stops.

The constraint: someone who has genuinely stopped taking work should still stay low until they take work again.

Known

Shown in the data and the code

  • Missed pings jumped on 12 Aug and stayed high.
  • A miss and a "no" cost the same, and the score never decays.
  • Four responders' scores fell to about zero, and their pings with them.
  • The ping wait did shrink: about 92 s between a miss and the next ping before, about 62 s after.
Not proven

Still open

  • Why the first misses happened. Shorter wait or changed weights? The data has no answer times.
  • People who answered in 60 to 90 seconds now missing fits the code but is untested.
  • "It's seasonal": no earlier-year data to check.
  • Whether treating decliners the same was a decision. The 14 Aug question has no recorded answer.

Act III · Develop · Chapter 5 · Super Speed

The Director asked for something real.

NarratorHelen did not want a number quietly changed. She wanted to see what we would build, from the point of view of the person it happens to. So we weighed the options.

OptionWhere it landed
Score recovery over timeChosen. Helps whichever 4.2 change caused the misses, and it has to answer the 2019 note's counter-argument.
Treat a miss differently from a "no"Considered. The code says they are the same on purpose, and the question to the staff engineer is unanswered.
Guarantee a way back inConsidered. Needs a rule for when a low-scored responder gets a ping.
Revert the 90-second waitNot recommended yet: we can't say which 4.2 change caused the misses.

Act IV · Deliver · Chapter 6 · Sidekicks

Two sidekicks that check the work before it ships.

NarratorA fix is only as good as the checking behind it. So we built two sidekicks: one that tests a brief, and one that tests a build. They do not stop a release by themselves. They make the gaps show up on paper first.

Sidekick 1

review-checklist: tests the brief

Run on my own brief:

✓ Success measureCounts responders pinged less than half as often.
✓ ScopeStays on score recovery.
✓ Problem firstProblem comes before the fix.
✗ OwnerNo one named as responsible.
✗ Ready for managementEnds in questions, not a decision.
✗ Plain languageScoring values up front.
The prompt I wrote (#2)
Make a skill called review-checklist. When I point it at a brief, it checks that the brief: names who owns it, says how we'll know it worked, keeps the same scope start to finish, and explains the problem before the fix.
Sidekick 2

qa-testing: tests the build

I approve acceptance criteria drawn from the brief, then the skill runs them in a real browser and writes a report.

18 / 19criteria passed, including every "must"
1caught a console that did not show Kip's responders; fixed and retested

Its checks include the edges: the 0.00 floor, the 1.00 ceiling, and that a miss costs exactly what a "no" costs.

The prompt I wrote (#6)
Make a skill called qa-testing. When I point to the product brief that outlines the requirements & the staging environment where the code build against it is housed, it creates robust acceptance criteria’s, validate’s them with me, performs the tests & generates a document with all findings.

Act IV · Deliver · Measure and next steps

Measure the people, not just the mean.

NarratorAnd how will we know it worked? Not by the weekly average. That is the number that hid the problem.

Success measure · from the brief

Count responders pinged less than half as often as before 4.2, and how many weeks a responder stays quiet. Ravi to confirm this can be reported per responder.

Guardrails · my judgment

Keep watching missed pings per week and the weekly acceptance rate, so recovery does not quietly raise misses elsewhere.

Gap

The brief sets no target and no owner yet. A target, and someone who owns the per-responder numbers, are both still to be agreed.

Before the brief goes to Helen

  • Name an owner for the work.
  • Replace the closing questions with one clear decision to approve.
  • Move scoring values out of the opening and say what Meteor Mite and Kip actually experience.

To close the open questions

  • Get answer times from the staff engineer and Ravi, so we can tell the 60-second wait from the weight change.
  • Get the 14 Aug question answered: was treating decliners the same a decision?
  • Check what the console shows handlers today before promising a "recovering" marker.
